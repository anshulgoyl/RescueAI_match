# Content writing guide

## Purpose

Write short, kind introductions that help a visitor decide whether to open the official profile. The official Dogs on a Journey profile remains the source of truth.

## Source rule

Use only facts confirmed in the current approved profile or by the adoption team. If a detail is absent, say it is not yet known or leave it out. Never infer temperament, compatibility, health, history, or training from a photograph.

## Card copy

- **Name and facts:** preserve the approved sex, age, size, and country.
- **One-line introduction:** 8–18 warm, factual words. Start with an observable or documented quality.
- **Match label:** use “Een mogelijke match” when the relevant information is known; use “Laten we samen kijken” when key suitability information is unknown.

Good: “Kalm en rustig; sociaal met honden en katten en loopt goed aan de lijn.”

Avoid: “Perfect family dog” or “Definitely loves children” unless the approved profile explicitly says so.

## Profile-dialog copy

Show three short details: social with dogs, social with cats, and with children. Use only:

- “Ja, volgens het profiel”
- “Nee, volgens het profiel”
- “Nog niet bekend”

Finish with a warm reminder: the full profile and adoption team can answer the questions that a short introduction cannot.

## Voice

Warm, patient, hopeful, and plain-spoken. Invite people to get to know a dog; do not pressure them to adopt. Prefer “maatje,” “leren kennen,” and “samen kijken” over administrative language such as “file,” “candidate,” or “eligibility.”
